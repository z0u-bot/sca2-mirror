# Stochastic rounding of off-grid answers

Raised in the ex-2.2.3 prereg review, from the grammar's rounding rule. `screen`, `multiply`, and (off its on-grid pairs) `mix` compute a per-channel value between two grid levels and snap it to the nearer one. A deterministic snap makes the task a step function of the raw value: 10 rounds to 9 and 11 to 12, every time. The alternative is to round stochastically, so 10 rounds to 9 two-thirds of the time and to 12 one-third, drawn per line at corpus build. The model would then be trained toward the interpolated answer distribution, and a well-calibrated model would spread its answer probability over the two neighbors in proportion to the distance, which reads the effective geometry rather than the snap.

Not taken in ex-2.2.3, because it changes what the task's exact-match ceiling is. A stochastic target puts the ceiling below 1 on every rounded op (the model cannot know which way a line's coin fell), and the calibration and H1 read holdout exact match against "near 1". It would also change every op's agreement and op-relevance figures from constants to distributions.

What it would buy, and what a grammar variant would have to settle: (a) the ceiling, and whether exact match is the wrong statistic under it (the answer-distance reads ex-2.2.3 adds are the alternative); (b) whether a stochastic target makes the answer representation more graded, in the sense of the [distance-shaped answer targets](./distance-shaped-answer-targets-ex-2-1.md) item, which measures that on a deterministic corpus; (c) whether it changes the redder-than-both counts and so E3. Ex-2.2.3's rule is the nearest level with `mix`'s ties sent to the even level index, so its mean signed rounding error is near zero over the pairs, which answers the bias half of the concern without a stochastic target.

## Notes

**2026-09-10, pilot (Fable)** — Ran as [pilot-stochastic-rounding](/docs/m2/pilot-stochastic-rounding/report.py), on the dev store: `Rounding = "stochastic"` in `sca.data.ops`, control and the adopted recipe on the stochastic corpus (two and three seeds) plus two fresh nearest-rounded control seeds, all at `recipe-short`'s length. (a) is settled: the model learns the rule's answer distribution (P(mode) matches the coin's odds to within 0.02 on every rounding op; KL from the rule 0.13–0.25 nats against 2.3–5.1 for the nearest-rounded control), so drawn-answer exact match sits at the holdout ceiling (mix 0.443, screen 0.637, multiply 0.652) and carries one shared draw's noise (about ±0.03 over 256 lines) that model seeds cannot average away. The reads to use under such a target are expected exact match against the holdout ceiling, calibration (P(mode) or KL), and exact match against the mode on ops without ties; on `mix` every off-grid channel is a half-way tie, so the mode read there is a coin toss. (c): the expected redder-than-both count rises by 14% on `screen` and 21% on `multiply` and is unchanged on `mix`, with no model in the loop. Anchoring does not notice the corpus (m_line 0.391 against production's 0.392 ± 0.020; every placement statistic in band); suppression reads shift through their clean baseline (clean red-line accuracy on `multiply`'s probe lines 0.88, since a calibrated argmax only lands on the nearest level where the mode is clear) and the redder lines hold their answers under projection at production's ratio. (b) is still open: the cube probes run on `mix`'s on-grid lines, which never round, so the pilot could not see gradedness; answering it needs probes on the rounded lines of `screen` or `multiply`, reading the answer distribution's spread against the raw value. Recommendation for D2.2: keep nearest rounding; the variant earns a place only when the question is about the answer's own representation.
